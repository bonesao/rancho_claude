## Sprays / treatments

**Poligrhesa (caldo sulfocálcico / lime sulfur) — timing corrected (2026-08-09)**
Product on hand: "Poligrhesa," insecticida-fungicida-acaricida concentrado
soluble. Label composition confirmed: azufre de polisulfuros 25g + azufre
de tiosulfatos 0.4–0.9g + agua e inertes c.s.p. 100cm³ — the textbook
signature of caldo sulfocálcico / polisulfuro de calcio (lime sulfur), the
traditional mineral dormant spray for stone fruit (durazno, ciruela,
pelón). Fits the low-industrial-input
approach fine (mineral, not synthetic), but was recommended for the wrong
window:
- **Correct timing: dormancy, before bud break** ("yema hinchada"/swollen
  bud, before petals open) — **not** during bloom. Lime sulfur is
  phytotoxic to open blossoms (burns petals/pistils, can reduce fruit set)
  and highly toxic to foraging bees.
- **Second round "when fruit starts growing" is also questionable** —
  concentrated lime sulfur on young fruitlets risks skin russeting/burn.
  If the underlying concern is brown rot (moniliasis) or curculio at
  fruit-set, that normally calls for a different, gentler product at a
  specific stage (petal fall, shuck split), not a repeat lime-sulfur pass.
- **2026 call**: if flowers are already open on a given tree, skip this
  round rather than risk fruit set/bees; treat it instead as next winter's
  dormant-season spray (before bud swell). Re-check with whoever
  recommended the fruit-set-stage round before doing it.
- **Durazno specifically (2026-08-09)**: only a few petals open, most buds
  still closed but clearly imminent. Considered spot-treating just the
  closed buds — **ruled out**: can't cleanly confine a spray to closed buds
  next to open ones (drift/overspray), buds this close to opening are past
  true dormancy and more burn-prone, and bees are already working the open
  flowers on the same tree regardless of where product lands. Skipped
  entirely for 2026. **Next year: start the dormant spray earlier**, at
  first bud swell before any color shows, not after bloom has begun.
- **Pelón (2026-08-09)**: same species as durazno (*P. persica*, fuzzless
  type), also already blooming — same reasoning, **skipped for 2026**.
  Same grafolita monitoring/cultural-control plan (pheromone trap,
  prune-and-destroy wilted shoot tips, fruit sanitation) extended to
  pelón, since it's equally vulnerable and untouched by the spray decision
  either way.
- **Ciruelo (2026-08-09)**: buds still small, green, closed — no petal
  color showing yet, i.e. likely still in the pre-bloom window (unlike
  durazno/pelón). **Recommended: apply Poligrhesa now**, promptly, since
  this is probably the last window before it also crosses into bloom —
  pending confirmation no color is showing on any buds before spraying. If
  color turns out to already be visible, the same skip-it logic as durazno
  applies instead.
- **Both durazno/pelón, no dormant spray this year**: watch new spring
  foliage for peach leaf curl (crespura, *Taphrina deformans* — puckered,
  reddish, distorted leaves). No effective rescue spray once it appears;
  purely a monitoring note to confirm next year's earlier timing matters.
  General non-spray housekeeping: thin fruit once set, keep fallen
  fruit/debris cleared from under both trees through the season.
## Mandarina — forcing fruiting (2026-08-18)

Only 2 fruits so far despite being big/bushy/healthy (already pruned) —
likely too much vegetative vigor diverting energy from flowering, though
tree is also only ~4 years since replanted from pot so some of this may
just be juvenile phase resolving on its own.

Considered 3 forcing techniques seen online: wire-ring girdling, branch
weighting/bending, bark stripping. All are real, mechanistically sound
(interrupting/redirecting phloem sugar flow or auxin balance to favor
flower buds over shoot growth) — but they trade root/tree vigor for
fruit, and the trade compounds if overused: chronic or repeated girdling
starves root growth (reduced drought/cold tolerance later — relevant
given this property's frost history), and the girdle scar is a permanent
structural weak point + disease/borer entry site even after healing.

**Plan**: branch bending only, on 1-2 vigorous branches, this season.
Reversible and lowest risk. Do **not** escalate to wire girdling unless
bending shows no effect after a full season — if escalating, only on
secondary branches (never trunk), remove wire promptly once response
seen. Bark stripping ruled out (permanent, worst risk/control ratio, no
advantage over wire girdling). Not repeating any forcing technique
annually — one-time bounded experiment, not a recurring practice.

## Manzana — chill-hours note (2026-08-09)

Full winter leaf drop is normal/expected for apple (deciduous, needs real
cold — that's *why* it drops leaves, not despite it). No issue observed
this year. Flagging for later: apple chill-hour requirements vary a lot by
variety (~200–1000+ hrs below 7°C), and Buenos Aires winters are mild, so
a mismatched variety is a plausible failure mode down the line. **Chill
can't be added retroactively** — no practical way to refrigerate an
outdoor tree, and chemical rest-breaking agents (hydrogen cyanamide/Dormex)
exist commercially but are toxic/restricted and not a fit for this
property's low-input approach. **Trigger to revisit**: weak, delayed, or
patchy bud break/bloom next spring — if that shows up, check the variety's
chill requirement and consider a known low-chill cultivar (e.g. Anna,
Dorsett Golden, Einshemer) as a long-term fix. No action needed now.

**Concrete case to watch (2026-08-09):** the new <2m tree is tagged **Red
Delicious**, commonly cited around 700–800 chill hours — on the higher
end, plausible mismatch for this climate. Not a confirmed issue, just the
one to watch specifically next spring per the trigger above. The older
~3m tree (unknown variety) is the natural comparison point — if it leafs
out/blooms normally while the Red Delicious lags, that's the tell.

## Apple varieties for future orchard expansion — cider goal (2026-08-09)

Goal: expand the apple planting eventually, with an eye toward homebrew
hard cider. Key tension: classic English/French cider-specific cultivars
(Dabinett, Kingston Black, Yarlington Mill, etc.) are bred for cold
climates, need more chill than this site gets, and aren't realistically
sourceable in Argentine nurseries. Practical path instead: **blend
low-chill dessert varieties (sugar) with at least one tart/crab apple
(acid + tannin)** rather than chasing imported single-purpose cider stock.

**Low-chill dessert varieties to look for** (established performers in
Brazil's/northern Argentina's mild-winter zones):
- **Anna** — very low chill (~200–300 hrs), reliable in mild climates.
- **Dorsett Golden** — ~200–250 hrs, and the classic pollination pair for
  Anna (shared bloom timing + chill need) — buying both solves variety
  selection and pollination at once.
- **Einshemer (Ein Shemer)** — ~250–350 hrs, precocious bearer.
- **Gala** — moderate-low chill, already common in Argentina, good sugar
  for fermenting.
- **Fuji** — moderate-low chill (strain-dependent), high sugar, widely
  available.

**For actual cider character**: the above are all sweet/low-tannin on
their own — look for a **manzano silvestre / crab apple** or any locally
available tart apple to blend in for acid/tannin balance. Ask viveros
specifically for "manzana ácida" or crab apple stock when expanding.

**Pollination**: apples generally aren't self-fertile — plan additions as
compatible pairs with overlapping bloom (Anna/Dorsett Golden is the easy
low-chill pair). Once bloom timing is observed, check whether the existing
Red Delicious and the unidentified older tree already cross-pollinate each
other.

**Durazno — suspected Grafolita (oriental fruit moth) (2026-08-09)**
Last season: amber gum exuding at petals/leaf tips, plus fruit that never
fully developed and came out malformed. Leading hypothesis is *Grapholita
molesta* (grafolita), the dominant durazno/ciruela pest in Argentina —
spring larvae tunnel into new shoot tips (wilted tip + gum = the resin
symptom seen), later generations bore into fruit (gummy frass at entry
point + malformation). Lime sulfur does **not** control this — separate
pest, separate plan. Alternate/secondary possibility: mosca de los frutos
(fruit fly), which stings ripening fruit but doesn't typically cause
shoot-tip gumming — the tip symptom is what points to grafolita specifically.
**Confirm next occurrence**: cut open a wilted tip or malformed fruit —
small pinkish-white caterpillar inside = confirmed.
**Plan for this season:**
- Hang a grafolita pheromone trap to confirm ID and time any spray to
  actual moth flight.
- As new shoots flush post-bloom, prune and destroy wilted tips promptly —
  kills the larva before it reaches a fruit-boring generation.
- Remove/destroy dropped or infested fruit away from the tree (larvae
  pupate in soil/mulch below).
- If pressure is heavy, spinosad timed to petal fall/shuck stage is the
  standard lower-toxicity option — not while bees are on open blossoms.

## Mamboretá K fungicida — label notes (2026-09-06)

Bought to treat scorched leaves on durazno/pelón (suspected torque, unconfirmed) and
the baby mango's excess-humidity issue. Pulled the official label (ARIPA S.R.L.
prospecto) rather than guess a dilution.

- **Active ingredient**: Captan 80g/100g (wettable powder). Protectant fungicide —
  sits on the leaf surface and blocks spore germination; the label does **not**
  claim systemic movement (one reseller listing said "sistémico," that appears to
  be wrong/copy-pasted from another product — don't trust it).
- **Mixing**: make a lump-free paste with a little water first, then top up to
  volume while stirring; use immediately, don't premix the day before.
- **Foliar application** (durazno, pelón, ciruelo, cerezo, etc.): spray to cover
  foliage (both leaf faces) to the point of dripping but not soaking/saturating.
  Repeat after heavy rain. **Not** a trunk-base drench for stone fruit — the
  label's trunk-painting method (base to 1–1.5m before winter rains) is a
  citrus-only use, for Phytophthora foot rot/gomosis, doesn't apply here.
- **Compatibility**: don't mix with, or apply close in time to, alkaline products
  or oils/emulsifiable insecticides (phytotoxicity risk) — relevant since
  Poligrhesa/lime sulfur is already in rotation on these trees; keep the two
  spaced apart, don't tank-mix.
- **Bee toxicity**: label states virtually non-toxic to bees. Very toxic to fish —
  avoid runoff into any water source.
- **Carencia (harvest interval)**: 14 days for duraznero/damasco/ciruelo/citrus.

**Durazno/pelón — dose by target disease** (per liter of water; "medida" = the
included 0.5g scoop):
| Disease | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Torque (*Taphrina deformans*) | 1.8g (4 medidas) | Al hincharse las yemas (bud swell) only — **no curative claim on the label for already-curled leaves**. This year's window already passed (leaves already scorched), so this application won't reverse existing damage — it mainly limits spread to any not-yet-affected new growth and reduces inoculum. |
| Podredumbre morena / brown rot (*Monilia fructícola*) | 1.5g (3 medidas) | Al cuajar el fruto (fruit set — i.e. **now**, September) and repeat until 30 days before ripening. Good timing match for a spray today. |
| Mal de la munición / shot hole (*Stigmina carpophila*) | 1.5g (3 medidas) | 3-stage: bud swell → petal fall → fruit hazelnut-sized, then every 15–20 days if weather favors it, plus a final application at leaf fall to cut inoculum for next season. |

**Practical call for today's spray**: use **1.5g/L (3 medidas)** — covers monilia
and shot hole, both actually timed for right now at fruit-set, and is close
enough to torque's own listed dose. Apply as a foliar spray per above; treat it
as damage control/prevention for the current scorched leaves, not a cure.

**Realistic cadence (2026-09-06)**: contact/protectant, not systemic — washes
off in rain, doesn't protect new growth, so the label's own rhythm is "every
15-20 days" or "repeat after heavy rain," not daily/weekly on a fixed clock.
Plan: 1st spray now (fruit set, covers monilia + shot hole together), repeat
every 15-20 days (shorter end if wet weather), re-spray after any strong rain
regardless of schedule, stop at least 30 days before fruit ripens and respect
the 14-day carencia before harvest — **2-4 applications total** this cycle.
One separate extra application at autumn leaf fall (~Apr/May) for shot-hole
inoculum, unrelated to this spring series. Mango root drench is one-off, not a
series — only repeat if the symptom persists after 1-2 weeks *and* the
drainage/watering fix is already in place.

**Bee safety / timing (2026-09-06)**: label lists captan as "virtually
non-toxic" to bees (unlike Poligrhesa/lime sulfur or neem, both flagged
elsewhere as bee-risky). Lower baseline risk, but bees are still actively
working durazno flowers right now, so the same precaution already used for the
jabón potásico/neem plan applies: **spray at dusk**, not midday — lets it dry
overnight before bees resume foraging, and avoids wetting any bee mid-forage
even though the product itself is low-risk. Aim the spray at foliage/fruit,
not directly into open blossoms, where practical.

**Baby mango — soil/root use**: label also lists Mamboretá K as a seedling
("almácigo/plantín") treatment against soil-fungi complex (damping-off:
*Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*) — labeled use is a **soil drench around the root
zone**, not foliage, for this purpose (different application mode than the
durazno spray above). Mix at the same ~1.5g/L rate and water it in around the
base, enough to wet the top ~1cm of soil (per the label's post-germination
outbreak instructions for almácigos). **This is secondary support, not the
fix** — per the existing plan for this tree, the excess-humidity problem needs
a drainage/watering correction first; the drench just guards against secondary
root rot while that's addressed.

Sources: [Mamboretá K official product page](https://mamboreta.com.ar/producto/mamboreta-k-fungicida/), [official label PDF](https://mamboreta.com.ar/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Prospecto-Mamboreta-K.pdf), [Mundo Huertas product listing](https://mundohuertas.com.ar/productos/mamboreta-k-fungicida/).

**Banana tea/peels — not a torque driver (2026-09-09)**

worsening the torque (Taphrina)? No — Taphrina only infects tender bud/leaf
tissue in the narrow bud-swell window under cool/wet weather; it isn't

support surface fungi like sooty mold in humid weather — separate issue from
torque. Practical tweak: keep peels off direct bark contact, prefer
soil-drench over foliar application if avoiding even that indirect risk.

## Early flower removal on young trees (2026-10-05)
Question: pluck blossoms on young apple (Red Delicious, <2 m, planted 2026-08) and cherry (~2 m, has branches) so energy goes to growth?
- Evidence: solid in commercial apple (Cornell/WSU/UMass practice: no fruit planting year, limited year 2; early cropping reduces trunk/canopy growth, proportional to crop load). Thinner for cherry (extrapolated). Not re-verified against specific papers.

Photo: dense cluster of small black/dark insects packed along a tender new shoot just below the growing tip, leaves at the tip curled/cupped and yellowing, pink flower buds right next to them. Ant-like dark dots among them are likely ants farming the colony (not confirmed in the photo).
- **ID: aphids (pulgones)**, most likely black citrus aphid (*Toxoptera aurantii*) or the spirea/cotton aphid (*Aphis spiraecola/gossypii*, dark green-black). Both only colonize soft spring flush, which is why they sit on the shoot tip and not on mature leaves. Distinguishing species doesn't change the treatment.
- Confirm: sticky "honeydew" shine on leaves below, ants walking up/down the trunk, white shed skins. Also check the curled leaves' undersides for silvery winding tracks (minador de los cítricos, a different pest that also curls leaves; if present, no spray, just prune).
- **Severity: low-moderate.** Healthy tree, localized. Main risks: distorted new growth on a tree still recovering from cold damage, sooty mold on honeydew, and virus spread (minor). Don't spray the whole tree for one infested shoot.
- **Next steps (least to most input):**
  1. Now: pinch off or prune the worst curled tip(s) into a bag, away from the tree. Squish or wipe the rest by hand; check all new shoots for other colonies.
  2. Strong water jet on shoots, morning, repeat every 2-3 days for ~2 weeks.
  3. Ants: if present, wrap the trunk with a sticky barrier (Tanglefoot-type or tape + grease, not touching bark directly), so ants stop protecting aphids and predators can work.
  4. Only if it keeps spreading: potassium soap (jabón potásico) 1-2% on the colonies, at dusk, contact only, repeat after 5-7 days. **Keep it off open flowers** (bees); pink buds not yet open are ok but avoid drenching them. Neem is a second option, same flower caution.
  5. Let predators in: ladybugs/syrphid flies/lacewings usually arrive within 1-3 weeks. Avoid broad insecticides. Alyssum or flowers nearby help.
- **Avoid:** nitrogen/seaweed/manure boosts to this tree this spring. Soft lush growth feeds aphids (and fits the earlier "don't stress a recovering tree" call in the product plan).
- **Re-check:** in ~1 week; look at the other citrus (naranja, mandarina, quinotos) for the same thing.
