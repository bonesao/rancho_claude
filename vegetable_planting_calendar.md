# Vegetable Planting Calendar

Planning reference for the homestead in **Loma Verde, Escobar**
(open pampas countryside, lat ~-34.35). Climate is humid subtropical
with a continental tilt — no urban heat island, more wind exposure,
flat terrain that lets cold air settle, and a notably longer frost
window than the city of Buenos Aires.

- First frosts typically arrive in **early-to-mid May**.
- Last frosts can linger to **mid-to-late September**, occasionally
  into early October in cold years.
- Treat **October 1 as the earliest safe date** for frost-tender
  transplants. The most heat-loving crops (pepper, eggplant) are safer
  going out mid-to-late October or even early November.
- Keep *manta térmica* (frost cloth / row cover) on hand through
  October for surprise nights, and again from mid-April onward for the
  autumn shoulder.

The greenhouse lets us pre-empt the season by ~6 weeks for warm-season
crops (tomato, pepper, eggplant, cucurbits), and to overwinter weak
seedlings.

**Frost pocket warning**: flat pampas land collects cold air; any
low-lying patch on the property will be 2-4 °C colder than the high
spots on frost nights. Site frost-tender beds on slight rises or near
walls / hedges that block cold air drainage.

## How to read the timeline

Legend:

- **S** — Start seedlings in greenhouse
- **T** — Transplant seedlings to bed
- **D** — Direct sow into bed
- **P** — Plant crowns / tubers / divisions
- **H** — Harvest window
- **·** — Crop in ground, growing (no action needed)

The seasons (Southern Hemisphere) are roughly:

```
   SUMMER          AUTUMN          WINTER          SPRING
Dec | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov
```

A few notes on reading the table:

- The greenhouse rows (S) are about 6 weeks before the corresponding T row.
- Many rows have two windows in the year — most leafy/cool crops can be
  grown twice (autumn-into-winter and late winter-into-spring), avoiding
  the deep heat of Dec-Feb.
- The high-summer hole for spinach, broccoli, radish and lettuce-likes
  is intentional: they bolt or get bitter above ~28 °C.

## Bed rotation reminder

To respect a 3- to 4-year rotation by family, group crops mentally as:

- **Solanaceae** — tomato, pepper, eggplant, potato
- **Cucurbits** — pumpkin, squash, luffa, zucchini
- **Legumes** — beans
- **Brassicas** — broccoli, kale
- **Chenopods** — spinach, acelga, beet
- **Asteraceae** — artichoke, sunchoke (both perennial-ish — get a permanent
  bed), sunflower (same family — keep this in mind for shared pest/disease
  pressure even though it's usually grown as a border plant, not rotated)
- **Liliaceae** — asparagus (perennial — gets a permanent bed)

Never plant a solanaceae bed back-to-back; tomato → bean → broccoli →
beet is a clean rotation cycle. Potato should not follow tomato either —
they share blight and verticillium.

---

## Soil pH note

The probe reading of ~pH 8 (alkaline) affects this list in two ways:

- **Potato** is the most sensitive — scab thrives in alkaline soil.
  Either acidify the potato bed locally (sulfur, pine needles, used
  coffee grounds) or expect cosmetic scab.
- **Iron / manganese chlorosis** can show up on tomato, bean and
  spinach as yellowing between leaf veins. If seen, a foliar spray of
  chelated iron is faster than amending the whole bed.
- **Asparagus and brassicas** actually prefer alkaline. Plant them in
  the worst (most alkaline) corners.

# Crop-by-crop notes

## Three Sisters (corn + climbing beans + pumpkin)

**Sequence is critical**: sow corn first (mid-to-late October in Loma
Verde, after the late-frost risk has passed), let it reach ~15 cm, then
plant beans at the base of each stalk, and pumpkin around the perimeter
of the patch. If beans go in at the same time as corn they outpace it
and smother the stalks.

- **Corn variety**: use a tall, sturdy maize (not sweet corn — sweet
  corn stalks are often too weak for the beans). Plant in blocks of at
  least 4×4 hills for pollination; a single row pollinates poorly.
- **Beans**: must be **pole / climbing** (e.g. *poroto pallar*,
  *chaucha vainita*), never bush beans — bush beans don't climb the
  corn, defeating the system.
- **Pumpkin**: any *Cucurbita* works, but Argentine varieties
  *anco / butternut* and *cabutiá* are reliable. The leaves shade out
  weeds and the prickly stems deter raccoons / opossums.
- **Watering**: heavy feeders. Water deeply 1-2× per week, mulch heavily.
- **Companion bonus**: nasturtium around the edges deters squash bugs.
- **Common mistake**: planting bush beans (already noted). Second most
  common: too-small corn patch → wind-pollinated corn produces empty
  cobs.

## Zucchini (zapallito)

Bush cucurbit — doesn't vine/climb like pumpkin or luffa, so no trellis or
tunnel needed. Fits standalone in its own bed or bag.

- Direct sow Oct-Nov once frost risk has passed. Fast maturity (45-55
  days) and cucurbits' general dislike of transplant disturbance both
  favor direct sowing over a greenhouse start.
- Optional early greenhouse start in September (same window as pumpkin/
  luffa) if an earlier harvest is wanted — not necessary otherwise.
- **Spacing**: give it room for airflow — one plant per small bed/bag.
  Dense cucurbit canopy is a powdery mildew risk, same concern as the
  mesh tunnel cucurbits.
- **Mistake to avoid**: letting fruit oversize — pick at 15-20 cm for best
  texture; oversized zucchini turns watery and seedy.

## Sunflower

Direct sow only — resents transplanting (long taproot), so a greenhouse
start isn't worthwhile.

- Sow Oct-Nov, same window as corn — works well as a border/companion
  planting near the Three Sisters patch or any bed that could use height
  or a windbreak.
- Same family (Asteraceae) as artichoke and sunchoke — keep that in mind
  for shared pests/disease if planted near either.
- Tall varieties can double as a temporary summer windbreak or shade line;
  site so the shadow doesn't fall on shorter, sun-hungry crops.

## Luffa

Vigorous climbing cucurbit — same trellising needs, timing, and pest
profile as the pumpkin/squash tunnel batch; grow it wherever a mesh arch
or other sturdy vertical support exists (see `mesh_tunnels.md`).

- Start in greenhouse Aug-Sep; transplant Oct-Nov once the support
  structure is physically in place — don't rush seedlings out to a bare
  trellis.
- Harvest young (15-20 cm) for eating like a summer squash, or let mature
  and dry on the vine into autumn for use as a scouring sponge.
- Heavy vine — needs a genuinely sturdy support, not a light stake.

## Artichoke

Perennial. Once established, a single plant produces 5-8 years.

- Propagate from offshoots ("hijuelos") rather than seed — faster and
  truer to type. Take divisions in autumn (Mar-May).
- Needs full sun and well-drained soil. Tolerates the alkaline pH well.
- **Cut back hard in late summer** (Feb) — leaves go ratty in the heat;
  the plant resprouts for the cool season.
- Heavy feeder: top-dress with compost twice a year.
- **Mistake to avoid**: leaving spent flower stalks on the plant — sap
  energy from the crown. Cut them at the base after harvest.

## Asparagus

The longest commitment in the garden — plant once for 15-20 years of
spears.

- Plant 1-year-old crowns in winter (Jun-Aug), in a trench 25-30 cm
  deep, backfilling progressively as shoots emerge.
- **Do not harvest in year 1**. Light harvest in year 2 (4 weeks only).
  Full harvest from year 3 onward (8-10 weeks each spring).
- Asparagus loves alkaline soil — this is the crop your pH is best for.
- Let the ferns grow tall after the harvest window — they feed next
  year's crown. Cut down to ground level in autumn after they yellow.
- **Mistake to avoid**: weed pressure. Once asparagus crowns are
  established they hate root disturbance, so weed the bed obsessively
  in years 1-2 to prevent perennial weeds from settling in.

## Sunchoke / Jerusalem artichoke (topinambur)

Tuber, Asteraceae family (same family as artichoke — not related to potato
despite the "artichoke" name). Extremely tolerant of poor soil, which makes
it one of the easiest fits for this property's profile.

- **Planting depth**: 10-15 cm, same as potato. Space tubers 30-45 cm apart.
- **Ideal pH**: quoted range is 5.5-7.5, but in practice it shrugs off
  alkaline soil without complaint — this is one of the few crops here that
  needs **no pH amendment** for our pH 8, unlike potato just below it.
- **Mulch**: yes, mulch after planting — it retains moisture and suppresses
  weeds while the shoots establish. Not needed for winter frost protection;
  tubers overwinter fine in the ground.
- **Give it a dedicated, permanent bed**, like artichoke and asparagus. Any
  tuber fragment left behind resprouts, so treat it as semi-permanent rather
  than part of the rotation — and site it where spreading won't be a
  problem, away from beds you till regularly. A contained raised bed or
  bolsón-style bag works well too — the walls physically limit the
  rhizomes, which is a reasonable alternative to a fully separate bed.
- Tall (1.5-3 m) sunflower-like foliage — same family, Asteraceae — doubles
  as a summer windbreak/shade line; plant on the north or west edge of a
  bed, not where it'll shade shorter crops.
- Yield drops with planting density: expect roughly 0.8-1.5 kg/plant in a
  tightly packed bed/bag vs. 1.5-3 kg/plant when well-spaced. Full
  hand-harvest is never 100% — missed fragments simply resprout next year.
- Harvest after the first frost kills back the foliage; dig tubers as
  needed through winter. They store better left in the ground than dug and
  kept indoors.

## Tomato

Warm-season, frost-tender, ~120 days seed-to-harvest.

- Start in greenhouse in **mid-July**; transplant after the last frost.
  In Loma Verde this means **early-to-mid October** for the first
  cohort, with a second cohort (started in September) going out in late
  October as insurance against a late frost wiping out the first.
- Stake or cage from day one — retrofitting supports tears roots.
- Prune indeterminate varieties to 1-2 main stems for airflow; humid
  Buenos Aires summers are *peronospora* and *tizón* heaven.
- **Companion**: basil and carrot — good. **Avoid** near brassicas,
  fennel, or potatoes (shared blight).
- Water at the base, never the leaves. Mulch.
- **Mistake to avoid**: overhead watering + dense planting = late
  blight wipeout by January. Space 60 cm minimum.

## Pepper (morrón / ají)

Like tomato but slower and more heat-loving. Soil must be **warm**
(>18 °C) before transplant — wait a week or two longer than tomato.

- Start in greenhouse in early August (a few weeks after tomato).
- **Transplant in November** in Loma Verde — wait for nights reliably
  above 12 °C. Soil temperature matters more than calendar date.
- Loves heat, hates cold nights — if a late frost threatens, cover with
  *manta térmica*. Peppers transplanted into a cold bed in October will
  sulk for a month and produce later than ones planted in November.
- Doesn't need staking but benefits from one in windy spots.
- **Companion**: tomato (similar needs), basil, carrot. Avoid with
  beans (allelopathic at close range).
- **Mistake to avoid**: transplanting too early. A pepper set out in
  cold soil sits there sulking until December.

## Eggplant

Same family and timing as tomato/pepper, but the most heat-loving of
the three.

- Start in greenhouse in August.
- Transplant only when nights are reliably >15 °C — in Loma Verde,
  realistically **early-to-mid November**.
- Stake; mature plants get heavy and crack.
- Pick fruits while shiny — dull skin = past prime, seeds harden.
- **Companion**: beans (the nitrogen-fixing helps), spinach, marigold
  (deters flea beetles).
- **Mistake to avoid**: letting one fruit over-mature on the plant
  signals "we're done here" to the plant and stops further set.

## Broccoli

Cool-season brassica. Two windows: autumn crop (start Jan-Feb) and
spring crop (start Jun-Jul).

- The autumn crop is easier — pest pressure is dropping; the spring
  crop races against summer heat.
- Soil pH 8 is *fine* for broccoli — alkaline soil suppresses clubroot,
  the worst brassica disease.
- Heavy feeder. Top-dress with compost at transplant and again when
  heads begin to form.
- After cutting the main head, **leave the plant in** — it produces
  side shoots for another 4-6 weeks.
- **Companion**: onion, beet, chard, lettuce, dill. Avoid tomato and
  strawberry (mutual antagonism).
- **Mistake to avoid**: planting too late in the spring window — once
  daytime temps hit 27 °C the heads "button" (stay tiny) or flower
  straight from a baby curd.

## Kale

Even more frost-hardy than broccoli — frost actually sweetens the leaves.
No greenhouse stage needed.

- Direct sow only, through the cool months (roughly Mar-Sep); pause
  through deep summer like the other brassicas.
- **Planting depth**: 1-1.5 cm, same shallow-sow rule as broccoli and
  other brassicas. Keep moist until germination (5-10 days).
- Harvest outer leaves like acelga/chard — central growing point left
  intact keeps the plant producing for months.
- Soil pH 8 is fine, same reasoning as broccoli — alkaline soil suppresses
  clubroot.
- **Companion / avoid**: same as broccoli (onion, beet, chard, dill good;
  avoid tomato, strawberry).

## Acelga (Swiss chard)

The most forgiving crop on this list — grows nearly year-round here.

- Direct sow; transplants OK but unnecessary.
- Soak seeds 4 hours before sowing for faster germination — each
  "seed" is actually a cluster, expect to thin.
- Harvest outer leaves, leave the central crown intact — a single plant
  produces for 6+ months.
- Bolts in deep summer (Dec-Jan); replant a fresh row in Feb for
  autumn-through-winter eating.
- **Companion**: nearly everything. Avoid only beans (mild allelopathy).
- **Mistake to avoid**: harvesting the central new leaves; the plant
  needs them to keep growing. Always pick from outside in.

## Spinach

Cool-season, bolts fast in heat. Tight window in our climate — mostly
an autumn-winter-early spring crop.

- Direct sow Mar-May (autumn) and Aug (late winter) for spring harvest.
- Soak seeds overnight before sowing.
- Soil-pH-sensitive: at pH 8, iron and manganese availability drops →
  watch for interveinal yellowing. A handful of compost at sowing
  buffers this.
- Picks like chard: outer leaves only.
- **Companion**: strawberry, brassicas, radish. Avoid potato.
- **Mistake to avoid**: sowing too late into spring. Once nights stay
  above 18 °C, spinach bolts within a week.

## Rabanito (radish)

The instant-gratification crop — 25-35 days seed to harvest.

- Direct sow successively (every 2 weeks) through the cool months;
  pause Dec-Feb.
- Use as a **bed marker / interplant** in slow-germinating beds
  (carrot, beet) — radish marks the row before the slower crops emerge.
- Useful sacrificial crop near cucurbits — draws flea beetles away.
- **Companion**: lettuce, peas, spinach, carrot, cucumber. Avoid
  hyssop and brassicas (close-quarters competition).
- **Mistake to avoid**: leaving them in the ground past maturity — they
  go woody and split within a week of being ready. Pick on time.

## Potatoes

Two planting windows: main crop **late August through September**
harvested in Nov-Dec, and a smaller autumn crop (Feb-Mar) harvested
before frosts. (The classic Buenos Aires "plant in July" advice gets
the emerging foliage burned by late August frost in open countryside —
plant a few weeks later and you lose nothing in yield.)

- Use **certified seed potato**, not supermarket spuds (which are
  treated with sprout inhibitor and may carry virus).
- "Chit" the seed potatoes (sprout them on a windowsill) for 2-3 weeks
  before planting.
- Plant 10-15 cm deep; hill up soil around the stems as they grow to
  increase yield.
- **The pH 8 issue is real here** — scab is more likely. Mitigations:
  acidify the trench with pine needles or sulfur, plant scab-resistant
  varieties (*Spunta*, *Kennebec*), and don't lime that bed.
- **Companion**: bean, corn, cabbage. Avoid tomato, pepper, eggplant,
  cucumber (shared diseases).
- **Mistake to avoid**: planting in a bed that had tomato or eggplant
  last year. Verticillium and blight will carry over.

## Beet

Cool-to-mild season; double-cropped like chard.

- Direct sow Feb-Apr and Aug-Oct. Soak seeds overnight; expect to thin
  (each "seed" is a cluster).
- Harvest at golf-ball to tennis-ball size — bigger gets woody.
- The greens are excellent eating (treat like chard — same family).
- **Companion**: onion, lettuce, brassicas. Avoid pole beans.
- **Mistake to avoid**: not thinning. Beets need 8-10 cm between plants
  or you get all tops, no root.

## Suggested rolling actions (by month)

This is meant to be a quick "what's overdue?" glance at any time of
year.

- **January**: harvest summer fruits, start broccoli seedlings for
  autumn crop, sow chard / beet / radish for autumn.
- **February**: cut artichokes hard, plant autumn potatoes, transplant
  autumn broccoli, sow spinach.
- **March-April**: artichoke divisions, sow chard / spinach / radish /
  beet, harvest summer crops.
- **May**: clean summer beds, plant garlic and onion (not in this list
  but worth flagging), final radish / spinach sowings.
- **June-July**: plant asparagus crowns, start tomato seeds in
  greenhouse (mid-July). *Hold off on main-crop potatoes — too early
  for Loma Verde.*
- **August**: start pepper / eggplant / pumpkin / broccoli seeds in
  greenhouse, plant main-crop potatoes from late August onward, prepare
  summer beds with compost.
- **September**: transplant broccoli, continue potato planting, start
  a second tomato cohort in the greenhouse as frost insurance, enjoy
  first asparagus / artichoke harvest. Do **not** transplant
  frost-tender crops yet — late frosts still possible.
- **October**: transplant tomato (first cohort), direct-sow corn and
  pole beans mid-to-late month, transplant pumpkin late October. Keep
  manta térmica accessible.
- **November**: transplant peppers, eggplant, second tomato cohort;
  weed obsessively, mulch heavily before summer heat.
- **December**: stay on top of watering and pest patrol; the garden is
  mostly in production, not planting.
