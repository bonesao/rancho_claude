# Document overview

This document tracks the current state of the vegetable beds plus a
running activity log. Updated whenever something is planted, harvested,
fails, or otherwise changes status.

Organized by **named growing area/sector** (as of 2026-09-11) rather than
by crop, since that's how the areas are tracked on the ground. A first
**schematic layout** (from the 2026-11-11 video walk-through) is in the next
section — adjacency only, no measurements or compass. These names stay the
source of truth.

For planting timing and per-crop notes, see
`vegetable_planting_calendar.md` (or `calendario_huerta.md` in Spanish).

# Notes / lessons to revisit

- **2026 tomato + pepper underperformance (earlier, undated planting)**:
  planted too late in summer/autumn to ripen before cooling temps slowed
  growth, and didn't survive winter. For future solanaceae: start seeds
  in greenhouse mid-July, transplant the first cohort early-to-mid
  October (per the planting calendar). The 2026-09-11 tomato/pepper
  sowing in the old raised bed is a fresh, unrelated planting.
- **Direct-sow vs. greenhouse-start for the bolsón batch**: skipping the
  greenhouse-start step for broccoli/eggplant/pepper/tomato in bags 1/2
  didn't visibly hurt broccoli/chard (bag 2) or beet (bag 1) (doing fine
  post-baby) — worth tracking eggplant/pepper/tomato outcomes here too before
  concluding direct sow is fine across the board.
- **Transplants beat direct seed in problem spots (2026-11)**: in both
  the compacted old raised bed and the failed tunnel round, ~10 cm tomato
  seedlings succeeded where direct-sown seed didn't. Counter-point to the
  direct-sow lesson above — for tomato (and likely other solanaceae) in
  difficult soil, transplant.
- **Weeding seedlings by mistake**: baby corn (and other grasses like
  amaranth/cereal seedlings) resemble grass weeds. Label sown rows with
  stakes and sow in straight lines so anything off-line is the weed.
- **Old bed compaction**: holes + topsoil on top weren't enough. Next
  year go deeper with aeration (broadfork / deep loosening once, then
  mulch + compost on top) — a one-off deep loosening isn't the same as
  regular tilling, so it still fits the no-till intent.
- **Broccoli vs chard in the same bag**: chard canopy shades broccoli
  out. Don't co-plant; give broccoli its own space or harvest chard
  harder/earlier.
- **Beet harvest**: unsure of timing — ask next session (typical 55–70
  days from sowing; big tubers can go woody, so check the largest one
  soon).
- **Seed saving (peas)**: leave pods until fully dry and rattling; store
  dry and labelled. Note the variety if known.
- **Kale (high raised bed)**: sown at an unlogged date, not seen growing.
  Check in person whether it's dead or just slow before writing it off.
