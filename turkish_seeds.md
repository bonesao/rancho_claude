# Turkish seed packet — ID + climate fit (2026-09-12)

Old packet (**~3 years old**, corrected from an initial ~2yr estimate) of
seeds bought in Turkey, labels partly in
Turkish/partly hard-to-read Spanish. Goal here: identify what each one
actually is and whether it's worth sowing given the Loma Verde/Escobar
climate (temperate, hot humid summers, mild winters with occasional
frost — same broad category as Turkey's own vegetable-growing regions,
so nothing here is a climate mismatch; it's mostly a *timing* question).

## Odd seed colors = seed treatment, not the plant

Three of the seven (aji largo, morrón rojo, remolacha) are described as a
color that doesn't match how that seed naturally looks (pepper seeds are
naturally pale cream/tan; beet seed clusters are naturally dull brown).
Bright red, pink, or blue coatings are a very standard commercial seed
treatment (fungicide, sometimes plus a colorant purely so the coated
batch is visually distinct from raw seed) — not a different variety or a
sign anything's wrong. Practical implications: wash hands after handling,
don't eat the seeds/coating itself, and it has no effect on the
vegetable once grown. It also isn't a species/type identifier — the true
identity comes from the printed name, not the color.

**Storage caveat**: the shelf-life figures above assume reasonably cool,
dark, dry storage. If this packet's actual storage was hotter/more humid
than that (common for a packet just kept in a drawer through Buenos Aires
summers), real viability could be meaningfully worse than these estimates
— another reason to test rather than assume.

## Bottom line

All seven grow fine in this climate — none of them are tropical or
otherwise mismatched. At 3 years old, seed-age risk is now spread across
more than just the carrot: purple carrot (#3) is the clear weak point,
the two peppers (#4, #5) and the two beans/legumes (#2, #6) are on the
low end of their typical range and worth testing, and roka (#1) and
white beetroot (#7) are still likely fine untested.

**None of these are exotic finds** — every one is a common, widely-grown
Mediterranean/temperate-basin vegetable (arugula, a borlotti-type bean, a
purple carrot variety, sweet peppers, cowpea, white beetroot) that's
readily available from local seed stores too, just sold under Turkish
names/varieties here. Worth treating this packet as ordinary seed stock
rather than something irreplaceable — no special preservation urgency
beyond the normal "test old seed before relying on it" caution above.
