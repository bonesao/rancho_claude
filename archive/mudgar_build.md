# Mudgar (Indian club / gada) from fallen eucalyptus

Status: planning — to resume. Source material = big eucalyptus branches that fell on the property.

## Goal
A swinging weighted club for **grip, wrist, and shoulder/joint** training. Function = an offset weight on a lever, swung behind the head. Two parameters matter: total weight, and how far the weight sits from the hand.

## Eucalyptus reality check
- **Pro:** dense and heavy (local species — *E. camaldulensis* red river gum, *E. globulus* — ~850–1050 kg/m³ dry, more green). Mass is what you want.
- **Con:** notorious for checking/cracking and warping as it dries.
  - Cracks on the **head** = cosmetic, harmless.
  - Cracks on the **handle** = SAFETY RISK. A handle that fails mid-swing launches the head. Handle integrity is non-negotiable — inspect for through-cracks before every use.
- **Carving tradeoff:** green euc carves relatively easily; seasoned euc is brutally hard on tools (interlocked grain → tearout). So "wait till dry, then carve" is the worst path.

## Options (lowest effort first)

### 1. MVP — ~20 min, usable today
Straightest knot-free section, ~4–5 cm thick × 70–90 cm long. Debark, smooth a grip end. Swing two-handed. Not a true gada (weight not concentrated at tip) but trains grip/wrist/shoulder. Start here while building the keeper.

### 2. Keeper — composite (an afternoon + cure) ← recommended
Use the densest fat chunk as the **head**, a *separate known-sound dry* piece as the **handle**.
- Rough out head from a thick section **green** (let it crack — fine on the head). Drill a socket, glue + wedge in the sound handle.
- OR cast a **concrete head**: mix concrete in a cheap plastic ball/bucket mould, set the handle into it, cure. Adjustable weight, no carving skill, handle integrity fully controlled. (One small bag of cement = minor one-time input.)

### 3. Full single-piece carve — most effort, highest crack risk
Only if you already have a well-seasoned piece, or accept rough-shaping green now + slow shade-drying with cut ends sealed (old paint / PVA / wax) to limit checking.

## Specs
- **Starting weight:** 2–4 kg total for grip/joint work (not the traditional 5–10 kg gada). Go heavier later.
- **Tools:** hatchet, drawknife or spokeshave, rasp/Surform, sandpaper; drill if composite.
- **Selection:** no knots in any piece that becomes the handle.

## Plan when resuming
1. Build option 1 to train this week.
2. Build option 2 (wood head + separate sound handle, or concrete head) as the keeper.
3. Skip option 3 unless carving for the enjoyment of it.
