Soil pH ~8 (alkaline) — three practical consequences

  • Potato is most sensitive — scab thrives in alkaline soil. Acidify the potato bed locally with sulfur, pine needles, or coffee grounds; pick scab-resistant varieties (Spunta, Kennebec); never lime that bed.
  • Iron / manganese chlorosis can show up on tomato, bean and spinach as yellowing between leaf veins. Foliar chelated iron is faster than amending the whole bed.
  • Asparagus and brassicas (broccoli) actually prefer alkaline. Plant them in the worst (most alkaline) corners — alkaline soil also suppresses clubroot, the worst brassica disease.

Three Sisters — featured polyculture

Corn + climbing beans + pumpkin. Plant in mid-to-late October in Loma Verde, after the late-frost risk has passed.

1 · Corn first

Tall, sturdy maize (not sweet corn — stalks too weak for beans). Plant in blocks of at least 4×4 hills for pollination. Wait until corn reaches ~15 cm before adding beans.

2 · Climbing beans

Must be pole / climbing (poroto pallar, chaucha vainita) — never bush beans, which won't climb the corn. Sow at the base of each stalk.

3 · Pumpkin around the edges

Any Cucurbita works; anco / butternut and cabutiá are reliable. Leaves shade out weeds; prickly stems deter raccoons. Nasturtium on the perimeter deters squash bugs.

Crop-by-crop notes

High-level considerations to keep in mind: companions, the single biggest mistake to avoid, and timing quirks specific to Loma Verde.

Corn 3 sisters

Greenhouse Aug-Sep · Direct sow Oct-Nov · Harvest Feb-Apr

Why it works here
Loves summer heat. Wind-pollinated, so plant in blocks, not rows — a single row produces empty cobs.
Companions
Beans (nitrogen), pumpkin (shade), nasturtium. Avoid tomato (shared earworm).
Mistake to avoid
Tiny patch → poor pollination → empty cobs. Minimum 4×4 hills.

Beans (pole) 3 sisters

Direct sow Oct-Nov · Harvest Feb-Apr

Variety matters
Must be pole / climbing (poroto pallar, chaucha vainita) — bush beans don't climb corn, defeating the system.
Companions
Corn, pumpkin, eggplant, beet. Avoid onion / garlic.
Mistake to avoid
Sowing beans the same day as corn → beans outpace and smother the stalks. Wait for corn to reach ~15 cm.

Pumpkin / squash 3 sisters

Greenhouse Aug-Sep · Transplant Oct-Nov · Harvest Feb-May

Variety
Anco / butternut and cabutiá are reliable Argentine varieties.
Care
Heavy feeders. Water deeply 1-2× weekly, mulch heavily. Pickly stems deter mammals.
Mistake to avoid
Transplanting too early into cold soil — they sit and sulk. Wait for warm October days.

Luffa cucurbit

Greenhouse Aug-Sep · Transplant Oct-Nov (once support exists) · Harvest Jan-Apr

Needs sturdy support
Vigorous climber — same trellising needs and timing as the pumpkin tunnel batch. Grow wherever a mesh arch or other firm vertical support exists.
Harvest
Young (15-20 cm) for eating like a summer squash, or mature/dry on the vine for a scouring sponge.
Mistake to avoid
Transplanting before the support structure is actually in place.

Zucchini (zapallito) cucurbit

Optional greenhouse Sep · Direct sow Oct-Nov · Harvest Dec-Mar

Habit
Bush, not vining — no trellis or tunnel needed. Fast maturity (45-55 days) favors direct sowing.
Spacing
One plant per small bed/bag for airflow — dense canopy is a powdery mildew risk.
Mistake to avoid
Letting fruit oversize. Pick at 15-20 cm — bigger turns watery and seedy.

Sunflower asteraceae

Direct sow Oct-Nov · Harvest Jan-Mar

Direct sow only
Resents transplanting (long taproot) — greenhouse start not worthwhile.
Use
Border/companion near corn or any bed wanting height or a windbreak. Same family as artichoke and sunchoke.
Siting
Tall varieties can shade a bed — plant where the shadow won't fall on shorter, sun-hungry crops.

Artichoke perennial

Propagate Mar-May · Harvest Sep-Jan

Propagation
From offshoots ("hijuelos") rather than seed — faster and truer to type. Take divisions in autumn.
Annual maintenance
Cut back hard in late summer (Feb) — leaves go ratty in heat; plant resprouts for cool season.
Mistake to avoid
Leaving spent flower stalks on the plant — they sap energy from the crown. Cut at base after harvest.

Sunchoke (topinambur) perennial

Plant tubers Aug-Sep · Harvest May-Aug (after first frost, dig through winter)

Planting
10-15 cm deep, same as potato. Space tubers 30-45 cm apart.
pH
Shrugs off alkaline pH 8 without amendment — unlike potato just below it.
Mulch
Mulch after planting to retain moisture and suppress weeds. Not needed for winter frost protection.
Give it a permanent home
Like artichoke and asparagus — any tuber fragment left behind resprouts. A contained bed or bolsón-style bag works well.

Asparagus perennial

Plant crowns Jun-Aug · Harvest Sep-Nov (from year 2-3)

Setup
Plant 1-year-old crowns in winter, in a trench 25-30 cm deep, backfilling as shoots emerge. The pH 8 soil is ideal here.
Patience
No harvest year 1. Light harvest year 2 (4 weeks only). Full harvest year 3+ (8-10 weeks each spring).
Mistake to avoid
Weed pressure in years 1-2 — once established, asparagus hates root disturbance. Weed obsessively early.

Tomato solanaceae

Greenhouse Jul-Sep · Transplant Oct-Nov · Harvest Dec-Apr

Loma Verde timing
Start mid-July; transplant first cohort early-to-mid October. Start a second cohort in September as insurance against late frost.
Care
Stake from day one — retrofitting tears roots. Prune indeterminates to 1-2 stems for airflow. Water at base only, never leaves.
Companions
Basil, carrot. Avoid brassicas, fennel, potato.
Mistake to avoid
Overhead watering + dense planting = late blight wipeout by January. Space 60 cm minimum.

Pepper (morrón / ají) solanaceae

Greenhouse Aug-Oct · Transplant November · Harvest Jan-Apr

Loma Verde timing
Wait until November — peppers in cold October soil will sulk for a month. Soil temperature matters more than calendar date.
Companions
Tomato (similar needs), basil, carrot. Avoid beans (allelopathic at close range).
Mistake to avoid
Transplanting too early. A pepper in cold soil produces later than one planted three weeks later in warm soil.

Eggplant solanaceae

Greenhouse Aug-Oct · Transplant November · Harvest Jan-Apr

Loma Verde timing
Most heat-loving of the three solanaceae. Wait for nights reliably above 15 °C — early-to-mid November.
Care
Stake — mature plants get heavy and crack. Pick while shiny; dull skin means past prime.
Companions
Beans (nitrogen), spinach, marigold (deters flea beetles).
Mistake to avoid
Letting one fruit over-mature signals "we're done" — plant stops further set. Pick aggressively.

Broccoli brassica

Two windows: Jan-Jul (autumn crop) and Jun-Oct (spring crop)

Loma Verde advantage
pH 8 soil suppresses clubroot — broccoli does very well here. Plant in your most alkaline bed.
Side shoots
After cutting the main head, leave the plant in — it produces side shoots for 4-6 more weeks.
Companions
Onion, beet, chard, lettuce, dill. Avoid tomato and strawberry.
Mistake to avoid
Late spring sowing — once daytime temps hit 27 °C, heads "button" or flower as tiny curds.

Kale brassica

Direct sow Mar-Sep · Harvest continuous through cool months

Frost-hardy
Even more so than broccoli — frost sweetens the leaves. No greenhouse stage needed.
Planting depth
1-1.5 cm, same shallow-sow rule as broccoli. Keep moist until germination (5-10 days).
Companions
Same as broccoli — onion, beet, chard, dill good; avoid tomato, strawberry.

Acelga (Swiss chard) chenopod

Sow Feb-Apr and Aug-Oct · Harvest continuous

Easiest crop
Grows nearly year-round here. Direct sow; soak seeds 4h. Each "seed" is a cluster — expect to thin.
Harvest method
Outer leaves only — central crown intact. A single plant produces for 6+ months.
Mistake to avoid
Harvesting central new leaves. Always pick from outside in.

Spinach chenopod

Sow Mar-May and Aug · Harvest Jun-Oct

Narrow window
Bolts fast in heat — mostly an autumn / winter / early spring crop. Soak seeds overnight before sowing.
pH 8 watch
Iron and manganese availability drops at pH 8 — watch for interveinal yellowing. Handful of compost at sowing buffers this.
Companions
Strawberry, brassicas, radish. Avoid potato.
Mistake to avoid
Sowing too late into spring — once nights stay above 18 °C, spinach bolts within a week.

Rabanito (radish) brassica

Sow successively Mar-Nov · Harvest 25-35 days later

Instant gratification
Direct sow every 2 weeks through cool months; pause Dec-Feb. Use as a bed-marker / interplant in slow-germinating rows.
Bonus function
Useful sacrificial crop near cucurbits — draws flea beetles away.
Mistake to avoid
Leaving in ground past maturity — woody and split within a week of being ready. Pick on time.

Potatoes solanaceae

Plant Aug-Sep (main) and Feb-Mar (autumn) · Harvest Nov-Dec and May-Jun

Loma Verde timing
Don't plant in July despite the classic Buenos Aires advice — emerging foliage gets burned by late August frost. Plant from late August onward.
Seed and setup
Use certified seed potato, not supermarket spuds. Chit (pre-sprout) 2-3 weeks before planting. Hill up soil as stems grow.
The pH 8 issue
Scab is more likely. Acidify the trench with pine needles or sulfur. Pick scab-resistant varieties (Spunta, Kennebec).
Mistake to avoid
Planting in a bed that had tomato or eggplant last year — verticillium and blight will carry over.

Beet chenopod

Sow Feb-Apr and Aug-Oct · Harvest 60-90 days

Two crops a year
Cool-to-mild season; double-cropped like chard. Soak seeds overnight; expect to thin (clusters).
Harvest size
Golf-ball to tennis-ball — bigger gets woody. Greens are excellent eating (same family as chard).
Companions
Onion, lettuce, brassicas. Avoid pole beans.
Mistake to avoid
Not thinning. Beets need 8-10 cm between plants or you get all tops, no root.

Suggested monthly actions

A quick "what's overdue?" glance for the weekly check-in.

January

Harvest summer fruits. Start broccoli seedlings for autumn crop. Sow chard / beet / radish for autumn.

February

Cut artichokes hard. Plant autumn potatoes. Transplant autumn broccoli. Sow spinach.

March-April

Artichoke divisions. Sow chard / spinach / radish / beet. Harvest tail end of summer crops. Keep manta térmica ready from mid-April.

May

Clean summer beds. Plant garlic and onion. Final radish / spinach sowings. First frost likely — cover anything tender.

June-July

Plant asparagus crowns. Start tomato seeds in greenhouse mid-July. Hold off on main-crop potatoes — too early for Loma Verde.

August

Start pepper / eggplant / pumpkin / broccoli seeds in greenhouse. Plant main-crop potatoes from late August. Prepare summer beds with compost.

September

Transplant broccoli. Continue potato planting. Start a second tomato cohort as frost insurance. First asparagus / artichoke harvest. Do not transplant frost-tender crops yet.

October

Transplant tomato (first cohort). Direct sow corn and pole beans mid-to-late month. Transplant pumpkin late October. Keep manta térmica accessible.

November

Transplant peppers, eggplant, second tomato cohort. Weed obsessively. Mulch heavily before summer heat sets in.

December

Stay on top of watering and pest patrol. Garden is mostly in production, not planting.
