# Avocado (Palta)

Monitoring project for 2 young avocado trees planted ~Jan–Feb 2026, ~70 cm tall,
in-ground under frost-cloth tents — **plus 4 younger "baby" avocados still in pots**,
not yet planted into the soil (see "Second batch" section below).
Location: Loma Verde, Escobar. See also [fruit_trees.md](fruit_trees.md).

## Key things to watch (short list)

- **Wet roots are the #1 killer.** Avocados are extremely sensitive to waterlogged soil (Phytophthora root rot). They want soil that drains fast and dries near the surface between waterings. "Mostly damp all the time" is a red flag, not a goal — especially in winter when the tree barely drinks.
- **Winter watering = very little.** Cool temps + mist + low evapotranspiration means the soil stays wet on its own. Water only when the top few cm are actually dry. Once a week is likely too much right now.
- **Frost is the other threat.** Young paltas are tender. Frost cloth tent + black water thermal batteries (good setup, see `avocado_01.jpg`) buffer the cold — that part you've handled well.
- **Ventilation.** A sealed tent + constant mist = high humidity, condensation, and mould (the white mould on the mango pot confirms it). Open the tent during the day for airflow; close it before nightfall when frost is expected.
- **Wind & sun.** Protect from strong wind and from harsh summer sun while small (shade cloth in heat waves — already done). Less of a concern now in winter.
- **Don't bury the trunk base.** Keep the root collar at soil level; mulch should not pile against the stem.

## Current concern (June 2026)

The avocado in the tent that **also holds the potted mango** is dropping leaves (drooping, then falling on touch). Likely causes, most to least probable given the conditions:

1. **Overwatering / poor drainage + low winter uptake** — soil damp for weeks, roots may be suffocating. This fits the symptoms (general droop + leaf drop) better than frost would.
2. **Trapped humidity / no ventilation** — white mould on the mango pot is direct evidence; stagnant moist air stresses the plant and invites root/crown rot.
3. **Winter cold stress** — possible, but frost cloth + thermal batteries should be buffering it; cold damage usually shows as browned/burnt leaf edges rather than whole-leaf drop.

**Actions taken:** opened the tent "door" for ventilation.

**Suggested next steps:**
- Stop watering this tent's tree until the top 3–5 cm of soil are dry; check by finger, not by surface look.
- Confirm the planting hole drains — if water pools, the tree may be sitting in a bowl. Consider a small drainage channel or raising/mounding later.
- Keep ventilating by day; only close up when a frost night is forecast.
- Move the mango pot out (or onto a saucer with airflow) so its wet soil/mould isn't adding humidity right next to the avocado.
- Watch the second avocado (other tent) as a control — if it's healthier on the same weather, the difference points to this tent's moisture/ventilation, not the season.

> Note: leaf drop in a young, recently-transplanted avocado isn't automatically fatal — they defoliated once already from transplant shock and recovered. Watch the stem/branches: if they stay green and firm, recovery is likely. Scratch a bit of bark — green underneath = alive.
